Inventory · 5 min read

Why inventory accuracy is the first fix

Many supply chain projects begin with new software or new warehouse layouts. Yet if the system says you have 100 units and the shelf has 80, every later decision rests on bad numbers.

Start with cycle counting. Count your high-value items every week, your medium items every month, and your low-value items every quarter. Fix the causes of each mismatch before adding anything new.

Last-mile · 4 min read

Five signs your last-mile costs are too high

Watch for repeat trips, failed first attempts, drivers returning with unused capacity, long gaps between drops, and cost per delivery rising faster than order volume.

Each sign has a practical fix, such as confirming time windows with customers, grouping drops by zone, and checking load plans before dispatch.

Planning · 6 min read

How S&OP brings sales, operations, and finance onto one plan

When sales forecasts, operations capacity, and finance budgets come from different sources, every department works from a different plan. The result is stockouts in one month and excess stock in the next.

A monthly sales and operations planning cycle, with one agreed forecast and named owners, removes most of that friction.

Container yards · 5 min read

Container yard congestion: where the time really goes

Long truck waits rarely come from the yard itself. Most time is lost at the gate, in incomplete paperwork, and in searching for containers that were stacked in the wrong place.

Moving document checks ahead of arrival, booking appointment slots, and recording container positions as they move can cut turnaround time significantly.
